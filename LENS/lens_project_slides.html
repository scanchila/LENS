Formal object

A candidate problem is a typed hypothesis.

It names the actor, the gap, the mechanism, the evidence, the assumptions, and the next test.

Theory

Rank with decision theory. Doubt with argument theory.

Ranking

  • Multi-attribute utility separates scale, urgency, tractability, and fit.
  • Value-focused thinking ties weights to the user's mandate.
  • Value of information points budget at tests that can change the ranking.
  • Exploration/exploitation reserves space for odd candidates while evidence is thin.

Evidence and opportunity

  • Opportunity theory asks who can act on the gap.
  • Creativity research keeps novelty separate from usefulness and feasibility.
  • Argumentation theory records grounds, warrants, rebuttals, and missing support.
  • Wicked-problem theory keeps the formulation open when facts conflict.

ITN/SNT, ODI, GRADE, CHNRI, TRL, RICE, and Heilmeier are adapters. The candidate definition comes first.
